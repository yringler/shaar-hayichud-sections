# Notes for the Rabbi Walters Original Class (`walters-original`)

What a writer of this series' study files needs to know beyond
`data/study/STYLE.md`. Catalog series 0, "Rabbi Benyamin Walters Original
Class". Written from chapter 10 (tapes 07-17); add to it as other chapters
are written.

## The classes

- Short tapes, about 8 to 17 minutes each, numbered in one run through the
  series. The order is not the book's: introduction (01-04), the cover page
  (05-06), chapters 10-15 (with a Q and A on 15), 16-22, a first addendum,
  23-24, then 1-2, 25-32 and a second addendum. Even so, in chapter 10 he
  speaks of chapters 1 to 9 as already learned ("as the Mitteler Rebbe
  pointed out in chapter 5").
- He speaks alone. No student is heard, there is no listener's "Right.",
  and in chapter 10 no student asks anything. The questions are his own,
  often put as an objection in dialogue ("You're going to say to me, 'Wait a
  minute...'"): `askedBy: "teacher"`.
- He reads the Hebrew himself, a phrase at a time, and gives the English at
  once, close to the words ("ומעתה, and now"), then explains. Sometimes he
  reads a whole line and goes straight into the explanation: those phrases
  keep their Hebrew and have no English. Where his only rendering of a line
  comes inside the explanation a few sentences on, a phrase may cite that
  sentence; say so in `notes`.
- Each tape picks up where the last one stopped. A tape often opens by
  summing up the previous one (a `recap`, placed under the passage it sums
  up, with `outOfPlace`) and can stop in the middle of a line.
- The transcripts have no speaker labels, and his Hebrew is the machine's
  hearing: where his reading differs from the chapter file, the chapter's
  lines are the text.
- Most tapes have Sofer's word times, not re-timed ones (the dump says
  "sofer times"); the seconds can be off, the word ranges are right.
- Now and then a remark to the recording itself ("I'll erase to the next
  slide", chapter 10): `omitted`, `logistics`.
- The chapter 10 tape titles end in "(M)"; the classes do not say what it
  stands for.

## His manner (keep it)

- Plain, warm, informal American English, spoken to "you". He builds a
  *mashal* into a little scene and acts out the dialogue: the nice guy with
  his hot cocoa and fuzzy slippers and the knock at the door; the two
  beggars on the park bench ("He gave me a coat." "I don't need a coat.");
  getting locked out with the coat; the cat stalking a mouse; "I ran out of
  passing-the-salt movements." Keep the props, the jokes and the dialogue.
- Science and technology, explained simply: the molecules and atoms of olive
  oil, the computer, the properties of water, nerves and the brain, a tree
  falling in the forest, color as frequency, the sun's fusion and the light
  bulb's filament, artificial intelligence ("You can't teach a computer
  humor"). Keep them, with his shrugs ("we don't really need to get into all
  the physics of it").
- History and everyday life: Napoleon as a baby, Mozart as a little kid, the
  eighth-grade class yearbook, basketball practice.
- He talks about method: where the Mitteler Rebbe is in his argument ("this
  is still the introduction to the chapter"), why there are several
  *meshalim*, that a *mashal* is to be understood physically and its
  *nimshal* not ("eat the fruit and throw out the peel"), what *v'dai
  l'mevin* asks of the reader. He sets up a paradox before he answers it
  ("Alarm should be going off in your head"). These are `keyPoint` blocks.
- Practical turns, short and pointed: *tzedaka* given with a sour face, "just
  be yourself". These are `avodah` blocks.
- Sources cited loosely and from memory (*Chovos HaLevavos*, *Tanya*, the
  Rambam, *Kuntres Inyana shel Toras HaChassidus*, *Mikdash Melech*). Report
  them as he gives them; a correction goes in `notes`.
- Hebrew terms inside English sentences (the *Atzmus*, *mamash*, *davka*);
  he says "G-d" far more often than *Hashem*.

## Naming him

"Rabbi Walters", and only where STYLE.md allows it.

## Translation

Phrases carry only his English as he gives it in class, each with the
`audio` of where he says it; a phrase he reads without translating has no
English (STYLE.md, Phrases). No published translation is used.

## Sensitivity, for this series

Flag as STYLE.md says. Chapter 10 is mild: one flag, his remark that a
charitable foundation is "a machine" that would let a person starve on the
doorstep (`other-groups`). "To mean that G-d is only kindness ... is
heresy" was left unflagged as a Torah position that names no group.
